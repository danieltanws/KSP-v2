# Water & Waste, global — the store holds only the response side

Skill #12 Gap analysis · run 16 Aug 2026 · **unapproved**

---

## 1. Evidence base

Based on **4 LAB documents (2022–2025)** and **12 LEAD actors**, 10 of them
linked by a matching document.

- **Geography:** Global (3 documents); Southeast Asia, South Asia, East Asia,
  Central Asia and Oceania (1 document, the ADB note, which spans all five)
- **Source types:** Policy 3, Research 1
- **No Media, Industry or Internal documents** in this scope
- **Publishers:** World Bank Group (2), Asian Development Bank (1), World
  Resources Institute (1)

Whole store: 4 documents, 12 actors. **This is a very thin base**, and #12 is
the lowest-trust analysis of the sixteen — the one most likely to reflect what
has been read rather than what is true.

## 2. Problem side

Three claims, and all three are made by an institution that also proposes the
response to them.

- 2.1 billion people lack safely managed drinking water; more than 3.4 billion
  live without adequate sanitation (JMP 2025); 4 billion experience water
  scarcity every year — *WBG Water Strategy Implementation Plan* (LAB)
- Water-dependent economic sectors provide 1.7 billion jobs worldwide, roughly
  half of all jobs globally — *WBG Water Strategy Implementation Plan* (LAB)
- Asia and the Pacific is the most disaster-prone region in the world, and
  water is the primary medium through which climate change impacts are felt —
  *ADB Mainstreaming Water Resilience Guidance Note* (LAB)

**No document in the store was written to establish that water is a problem.**
Each of these figures appears inside a strategy or guidance note, as the
premise for what that institution intends to do.

## 3. Response side

**From LAB** — three documented institutional responses, all recent:

- Seven scalable solutions, targeting water security for 400 million people by
  2030 — *WBG Water Strategy Implementation Plan* (LAB)
- Three pillars (Water for People, Food, Planet), with named private-capital
  levers: hybrid PPPs for wastewater reuse, sustainability-linked and
  catastrophe bonds, green securitization, catchment funds, pooled risk
  facilities, carbon market linkages, parametric insurance — *WBG Water
  Strategy FY25-30* (LAB)
- Six pillars for mainstreaming resilience across ADB water operations, plus
  three named initiatives as of April 2022: cluster technical assistance, the
  Asia and the Pacific Water Resilience Hub, and the RUWR initiative — *ADB
  Guidance Note* (LAB)
- A measurement framework rather than a response: 13 water risk indicators
  across quantity, quality and reputational concerns, running since 2011 —
  *WRI Aqueduct 4.0* (LAB)

**From LEAD** — 10 actors, and the mix is narrow:

- 2 Funders: World Bank Group, Asian Development Bank
- 1 Researcher organisation: World Resources Institute
- 7 individual Researchers, all authors of the single Aqueduct note
- **No implementers. No governments. No advocates.**

## 4. The gap

**Every document in this store is a funder or a researcher describing its own
intentions or its own instrument, so the store can say in detail what three
multilateral institutions plan to do about water and cannot say, from any
independent source, what problem that plan is meeting.**

This is the inverse of the asymmetry #12 exists to find. The response side is
unusually well evidenced; the problem side exists only as premises quoted
inside those same responses.

**No candidate follows from this.** Producing one would mean treating three
institutional strategies as evidence of a hole they do not fill.

## 5. What is missing

`Coverage gap` — not confirmed absence, and not close to it.

- **No independent problem evidence.** Every problem claim is quoted inside a
  response document by its author. Nothing here was written by someone
  observing a water problem rather than proposing to fund one.
- **No country-level evidence at all.** Three documents are Global; the fourth
  spans five regions. Not one names a single country, so no geography-specific
  question can be answered.
- **No Media, Industry or Internal documents.** Everything is Policy or
  Research from a multilateral or a think tank.
- **No implementers, governments or advocates in LEAD.** Ten of twelve actors
  are funders or researchers, and seven of those are co-authors of one paper.
- **Nothing on outcomes.** Three strategies state targets; no document reports
  whether any earlier one was met.

None of this says the world lacks these things. It says nobody has filed them.

## 6. What would change this

This would not hold if the store held one country-level assessment written by
someone other than a funder — at which point a real problem side would exist to
compare the three strategies against.

## Disclosures

**Problem/response classification is inferred, not stored**, and may sort
differently on another run. On this run: the WBG Implementation Plan and the
ADB Guidance Note were placed on **both** sides, because each states problem
figures as the premise for its own response. WBG Strategy FY25-30 is
response-only. Aqueduct 4.0 is neither — it is instrumentation, and forcing it
onto a side would misrepresent it.

**Nothing here is a recommendation.** It states what the store can and cannot
support.
